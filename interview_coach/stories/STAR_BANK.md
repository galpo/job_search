# STAR Stories Bank — Ritee Rouf
*Reusable interview stories keyed to competencies. Pull from these for any interview.*

---

## How to Use This
Each story is structured: **Situation → Task → Action → Result**
Pick the most relevant 2-3 for each interview. Adapt the emphasis to the company's stage and priorities.

---

## Story 1 — 0-to-1 Build
**Competency:** Building from scratch, founder mindset
**Company context:** LatticeFlow AI (2023–2024)

**S:** Joined LatticeFlow as Head of Marketing. Zero marketing function — no team, no budget, no process, no brand presence. Company had strong product but no GTM motion.

**T:** Build the entire marketing function from scratch while simultaneously supporting sales pipeline and raising brand awareness in a crowded AI space.

**A:** Hired and onboarded first marketing team members. Built messaging framework and ICP from ground up. Organized Davos AI House to create enterprise credibility. Launched press coverage including Forrester analyst relationship (Diego Lo Giudice). Created pipeline programs that directly influenced sales.

**R:** 35% pipeline growth. Established brand presence at Davos (AI House). Built the playbook that the team ran after me.

**Use when asked:** "Tell me about a time you built something from scratch." / "Describe your experience in early-stage companies."

## Story 2 — Category Creation
**Competency:** Narrative strategy, market shaping
**Company context:** Alation (2017–2018)

**S:** Alation had a product that didn't fit any existing category. Buyers didn't know what to call it. Competitors were calling it "data governance" or "metadata management" — both wrong frames.

**T:** Create and own a new category name that would become the default term in the market.

**A:** Led the positioning work to coin and launch "Data Catalog" as a category. Built analyst relationships (Gartner, Forrester) to get the term into reports. Aligned product, sales, and exec messaging. Created content and campaigns that used the term consistently.

**R:** "Data Catalog" became the standard category term in the industry. 7x SQL pipeline growth during my tenure.

**Use when asked:** "What does category creation mean to you?" / "Have you ever had to define a new market?"

## Story 3 — Executive Influence / Big Stage
**Competency:** Executive storytelling, cross-functional leadership
**Company context:** VMware (2015–2017)

**S:** VMworld — 20,000 attendees, the biggest enterprise infrastructure conference of the year. The keynote narrative had to serve multiple audiences: customers, partners, analysts, press.

**T:** Build the product marketing narrative that drove the VMworld keynote, representing the vSphere $4B portfolio.

**A:** Partnered with the CTO office and product leadership to shape the story. Translated deeply technical container/virtualization concepts into business outcomes. Wrote and rehearsed messaging with executives. Coordinated global launch timing.

**R:** VMworld keynote delivered to 20K attendees. vSphere Integrated Containers launched globally. Story was picked up by major tech press.

**Use when asked:** "Tell me about a time you influenced executives." / "Describe your largest launch."

## Story 4 — Revenue Impact
**Competency:** Revenue-connected PMM, repositioning
**Company context:** Omnicell (2020–2023)

**S:** Omnicell had a legacy hardware product (medication dispensing cabinets) that the market saw as a one-time capital purchase. The company needed to reposition toward XaaS (subscription/services) to improve recurring revenue metrics.

**T:** Reposition the product and messaging to shift buyer perception from CapEx to OpEx/subscription model.

**A:** Rebuilt the messaging framework. Created sales enablement that trained reps on the new value narrative. Worked with finance and product to define and communicate the XaaS offer. Led customer-facing content refresh.

**R:** $45M revenue is not tied to the XaaS repositioning. Pre-configured dispensing cabinets drove an additional $45M revenue growth during Covid. Xaas repositioning led to contract deals with 3 major healthcare providers including Stanford, Cleveland Clinic

**Use when asked:** "How do you connect PMM work to revenue?" / "Have you repositioned a product?"

## Story 5 — Developer / Technical Marketing
**Competency:** Developer audience, technical product, API/platform marketing
**Company context:** Google / Keyhole acquisition (2003–2008)

**S:** Google Earth was a consumer breakthrough — 200M+ installs. But the underlying mapping platform had enormous developer potential that wasn't being unlocked.

**T:** Launch and grow the Google Maps API developer ecosystem while maintaining the consumer brand.

**A:** Part of the founding team Keyhole pre-acquisition. Helped name and launch Google Earth (consumer) and contributed to the Maps API developer program. Co-organized the inaugural Google I/O — the first developer conference that became Google's flagship event.

**R:** 200M+ Google Earth installs. Google Maps API became the dominant mapping platform for developers. Google I/O is now a flagship annual event.

**Use when asked:** "Do you have experience with developer marketing?" / "Tell me about a product launch with massive scale."

## Story 6 — Cross-Functional Leadership
**Competency:** Working across product, sales, engineering, exec
**Company context:** VMware (2015–2017) — vSphere Integrated Containers

**S:** vSphere Integrated Containers was a net-new product in a rapidly evolving container space. It required coordination across engineering (Palo Alto), product (multiple BUs), sales (global field), and exec (CTO office).

**T:** Lead the go-to-market for a technically complex product to a technically savvy buyer base, while managing competing internal stakeholder priorities.

**A:** Built the GTM plan from scratch. Created messaging that worked for both IT buyers and developers. Aligned sales plays with regional field teams. Coordinated PR/analyst outreach. Ran the launch timeline across three geographies.

**R:** Global launch of vSphere Integrated Containers. Product adopted by enterprise accounts as bridge between legacy infra and container-native workloads.

**Use when asked:** "How do you work with engineering or product?" / "Describe managing a complex cross-functional launch."
