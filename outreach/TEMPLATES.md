# Outreach Templates

> What's actually working. Based on the Adam/ID.me model (warm Slack → replied within 24hrs → full interview loop).

---

## Template 1 — Direct to Hiring Manager (LinkedIn)
*Best for: warm 1st-degree connections or when you can find their email*

**Subject / opener:**
> "Your [role] description reads like my career philosophy"
> "This isn't a normal PMM application" ← mirrors company's own voice
> "Fellow San Mateo neighbor + your [role]"
> "One AI-first marketer to another"

**Message structure (4–5 bullets, scannable on mobile):**
```
Hi [Name],

[One sentence: why this role, why now, why you specifically.]

A few things that might be relevant:
• [Career highlight #1 — metric-driven, relevant to their stage]
• [Career highlight #2 — AI/GTM credibility]
• [Proof point — Substack, portfolio link, or specific work]
• [Local angle or personal connection if applicable]

[Soft CTA: coffee, quick call, or happy to send resume.]

[Name]
[LinkedIn URL]
```

**Rules:**
- Warm, confident — not salesy or desperate
- Lead with the specific role and why their JD resonated
- Substack link as proof you think in public
- Never more than 5 bullets
- Mobile-readable — short lines

## Template 2 — Peer-to-Peer / Networking (LinkedIn)
*Best for: PMM peers, people in roles you want, informational chats*

```
Hi [Name],

I came across your work on [specific thing — blog post, talk, company] and really appreciated [specific detail].

I'm a [title] with background in [2–3 relevant areas]. Would love to swap notes on [specific topic — GTM for AI, developer marketing, etc.] if you have 20 mins sometime.

No agenda — just a peer conversation.

[Name]
```

**Rules:**
- Be specific about what you read/saw — generic notes get ignored
- "No agenda" signals you're not asking for a job
- Keep it under 5 sentences

## Template 3 — Referral Ask (to a mutual connection)
*Best for: asking someone to introduce you to a hiring manager*

[one sentence on why].

[Name]

## Template 5 — Post-Interview Thank-You
*Send within 24 hours of any interview. Brief, specific, no over-thanking.*

```
Hi [Name],

Really enjoyed our conversation today — [one specific thing you discussed or that stood out].

[One sentence connecting your background to what they need.]

Looking forward to next steps.

[Name]
```

**Rules:**
- Reference something specific from the conversation (shows you were present)
- Don't say "Thank you SO much" — say "Thanks for the time" or just reference the conversation
- Keep it under 5 sentences

## Template 6 — Post-Rejection / Graceful Exit
*For when they say no, the role is filled, or you're moving on. Keeps the relationship warm.*

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for letting me know — I appreciate the transparency.

I've followed [Company]'s work for a while and think what you're building is genuinely interesting. 
If there's ever a role that might be a fit down the road, I'd love to reconnect.

In the meantime I'll be following along.

[Name]
[LinkedIn]
```

**Rules:**
- No bitterness, no fishing for feedback (unless you have a strong relationship)
- Short — they've moved on, so should you
- Leave a door open without being needy

## Template 7 — VP of People / Recruiter
*Best for: HR leaders, Talent Acquisition, Head of People. They care about fit, level, and timeline — not your product narrative.*

**Subject / opener:**
> "PMM (Sr. Director level) — available now, Bay Area"
> "Re: PMM role at [Company] — Sr. Director background, AI GTM"

**Message structure:**
```
Hi [Name],

I came across the [Role] opening at [Company] and wanted to reach out directly.

Quick overview:
• [Level]: Sr. Director / VP Product Marketing — [X] years enterprise B2B
• [Fit]: [One sentence connecting your background to the role]
• [Location]: San Mateo, CA — Bay Area based, no relocation needed
• [Timeline]: Available [immediately / within X weeks]

Resume: [link or "happy to send"]
LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/riteerouf

Happy to connect for a quick screen whenever works.

Ritee
```

**Rules:**
- Lead with level and availability — recruiters filter on this first
- Skip the product narrative and storytelling — save that for the hiring manager
- Include location explicitly (saves a screening question)
- Keep it under 100 words
- Always offer to send resume upfront
